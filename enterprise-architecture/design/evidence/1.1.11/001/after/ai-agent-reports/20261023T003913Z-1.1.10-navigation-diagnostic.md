### Why both records exist

This predates 1.1.10. The 1.1.5 ARB adjudication established **214 historical
Capability correspondences: 209 direct and five evolved**. Historical UUIDs and
Exchange representation IDs remain reserved for provenance and existing authored
Views, with explicit successor/predecessor correspondence. The current resource
supplies the governed meaning; an absent original definition stays absent.

The ARB expressly says a directly corresponding pair must not become two
independent current architectural capabilities merely because migration UUIDs
differ, and requires presentation of one coherent architectural concept. Historical
identity preservation is meaningful. A second current responsibility is not
established by that preservation. The two differently named evolved cases are HIE
Enablement and Care Appropriateness Review; the full correspondence evidence is
retained in [related cases][related]. No identity reconciliation is performed here.

### Why navigation emits both as subjects

The 1.1.10 classification was performed once into canonical `navigation.json`.
The [remap evidence][remap] records the before/after paths and basis for every
element. Current capability ancestry uses recorded parent/owner keys; historical
ancestry uses preserved original organisation. The remap removed standing/history
folders and required all 1,602 IDs to occur once in architectural scopes. It did
not distinguish “reserved historical identity” from “current subject” in the
numbered reading spine. Both CT1 roots therefore became siblings in Business or
Business Enabling scope, with their respective children retained.

The maintained `continuum_navigation.load` does not discover or deduplicate
capabilities. It consumes that explicit tree, resolves both titles from each
resource's displayName and assigns sibling-index coordinates. `project`, Manifest
and RAD then reproduce those labels. This is **canonical navigation modelling and
derivation**, faithfully amplified by publication; it is not an accidental second
PDF occurrence of the same element. The historical bodies visibly say superseded,
but outline/heading labels omit that distinction and the RAD abbreviates history
to a canonical-source link. The explicit successor in the historical canonical
record is not exposed as a coherent subject/back-reference in that abbreviated RAD.

The observed CT1 peer order is name order with ID order for same-name entries,
not a deliberate current-before-history order. Some superseded entries precede
their successors and others follow. The same derivation exposes the problem
below CT1: there are 85 CT2 and 103 CT3 exact-name groups, generally on corresponding
but separately numbered historical/current ancestry paths.

This is a reader-facing tension with the ARB coherent-concept treatment, preserved
for adjudication under AX-17. It is **not evidence that 1.1.10 changed semantic
standing or created another current Capability architecture**. Nor does diagnosis
authorise merging or discarding reserved identities.

### Why Stakeholders became peers

The implementation interpreted cross-cutting as a presentation *kind*, plus
avoiding a single heading literally named “Stakeholders.” It appended ten
individually numbered profiles as immediate children of Motivation. The source
has no cross-cutting structural placement separate from the subject spine. The
validator checks that perspective nodes are native Stakeholders in Motivation,
but not that their position communicates a cross-cutting perspective. The test
`test_numbering_covers_every_node_and_qualified_population` positively requires
all ten perspectives to be immediate Motivation children.

The root Manifest adds “cross-cutting perspective” to those same-level outline
entries. The Exchange organisation likewise has same-level element items. The RAD
renders a bold “Cross-cutting Motivation perspectives” caption and bold profiles,
using `pdfbookmark[0]`; in the actual PDF they have the same outline depth/parent
as I.1–I.8. The caption is unnumbered and is not an enclosing outline node. Profiles
are omitted from the printed contents, because their special path does not use
`addcontentsline`, while the full PDF outline includes them as peers. Despite code
and structure metadata calling this the Motivation “introduction,” the renderer
appends it **after** the ordinary chapters and Unclassified Motivation.

This is primarily canonical navigation modelling/interpretation of cross-cutting,
with publication choices that preserve peer outline placement. No new Stakeholder
associations arose from that choice. It fails to convey the agreed perspective
semantics even though no peer type-bucket chapter named “Stakeholders” was created.

## 3. Business Behaviour contextual preambles

### What is correctly contextual

No canonical Capability ID is located as an element beneath Business. Each of the
72 nodes has `contextCanonicalId`, referencing an existing Strategy Capability,
and has no `canonicalId` of its own. There is no new Capability instance or
semantic containment edge. Its label is supplied by the referenced resource.
All nodes are implicitly `kind=section`, rather than explicitly tagged references.

In every RAD body entry, the first content after the heading is:

> Capability context: [canonical capability name] ([Strategy coordinate]). The Strategy hierarchy remains authoritative.

The name is a LaTeX `hyperref` to the existing Strategy element; the Strategy
coordinate is printed explicitly. Thus the **RAD body already satisfies the
canonical-reference distinction for the context actually shown**. The [rendered
Business page][business-image] shows III.4.1, its Strategy reference II.1.3.11.1,
and the actual Service/Function descriptions. The diagnostic must not call that
legitimate contextual repetition a duplicate Capability model.

### What ancestry is missing

All 72 are immediate children of III.4. None has a contextual capability parent:

| Context kind | Count | Actual path | Omitted proper capability ancestry |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CT2 | 41 | Business Behaviour → CT2 → populated behaviour types | CT1 |
| CT3 | 29 | Business Behaviour → CT3 → populated behaviour types | CT1 and CT2 |

The labels are grouped by the `[CT2]`, `[CT3]`, `[Feature]` display prefixes and

reference does not supply the missing contextual ancestry to orient the reader.
CT2 nodes are present, but not beneath their CT1; CT3 nodes are present, but not
beneath their CT2. Therefore **none implements the intended complete relevant
contextual path**. This is flattening in canonical navigation, not a PDF-only
flattening. The full [context table][business] lists the omitted canonical ancestor
IDs, coordinates, tiers and standing for every occurrence.

For example, III.4.1 references CT2 Bed & Care-Place Management, whose Strategy path
includes CT1 Health Service Operations Enablement at II.1.3.11. That CT1 context
is omitted. III.4.70 references CT3 Workflow & Activity Coordination at
II.1.3.12.2.3, omitting CT1 Human Collaboration & Work Enablement and CT2 Operational
Activity & Temporal Enablement. Both ancestors are already superseded in the
canonical source; copying them silently as current context would hide another
standing issue rather than resolve it. III.4.71 and III.4.72 are Feature contexts
under Strategy CT3 Person Identity Administration; they omit all three CT ancestors.

A CT2-owned behaviour need not be assigned an invented CT3 simply to make every
path the same length. The required correction decision concerns relevant existing
ancestry and explicitly retained allocation depth, with no lexical reallocation.
